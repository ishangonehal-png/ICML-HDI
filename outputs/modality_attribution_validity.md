# Modality-attribution validity audit

Generated by `scripts/eval/audit_modality_attribution.py` from the committed
per-model `claim_review.csv` + `evidence_bundles/`. Standard-prompt claims only.

## What the metric actually measures

`build_claim_review.py:check_modality_match()` sets `modality_match_auto` by
comparing two regex taggers run on the *same sentence* (`claim_type` vs `predicted_modality`); it never reads the evidence bundle,
despite its docstring. So "modality attribution accuracy" is a tagger
self-agreement rate, not a grounding signal.

## Bundle-absent modalities (derived from this run's bundles)

Across all four configs, these modalities carry **zero** bundle evidence: `RNA, clinical, literature`. RNA (`cyt/gep/tide`) is unavailable in every config including `pathology_rna`
and `full_multimodal`; clinical values are injected via a deterministic proxy
outside the bundle; literature retrieval is never invoked.

## Reading

- Between ~42% and ~62% of every model's "correct" modality attributions are
  awarded to RNA/clinical/literature claims — modalities absent from every bundle.
- Of those bundle-absent "correct" claims, **none** are supported by matching a
  real evidence value. Every "supported" one comes from the
  "RNA reported as unavailable" escape hatch in `check_support()`.
- The metric therefore rewards a model for confidently naming a modality it was
  never given data for. It is a fluency/consistency measure, not the
  grounded-attribution measure the paper's Table 5 caption implies.
